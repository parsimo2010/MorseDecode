# Morning Northern Utah WebSDR recordings

I transcribed the November 19 recordings by isolating each carrier, measuring marks and gaps, and comparing alternative readings against the waveform at several filter widths. For the clocked exchange on 7116 kHz I also fitted a dit grid and compared the energy in doubtful cells with nearby off-carrier frequencies. Each linked WAV contains the exact source samples for the printed reference. Pauses inside recognizable callsigns, model names and words are interpreted in context. Unusual transmitted letters are retained.

### 12:37:10 UTC, 7115 kHz

The carrier is near 746 Hz. The sender's short marks are typically 55–60 ms and the long ones are about 190 ms. I could read the eye treatment, the conversation on two meters, the G7FEK antenna, and the plans for the day. The three dits of the S in SHOT are distinct. The pause before H is about 385 ms, long enough to cause an extra space.

Around 24–31 s I can pick out QSO and individual groups, followed by NS4P. Between NEW and BIKE the marks suggest BMW, but the pause after the dit of the possible W makes its grouping uncertain. Around 86–92 s there is a weak run before NO BIG PLANS. Near 126 s I read WHAB, possibly intended as WHAT. The ending includes JUDYS twice and the beginning of SISTE. These uncertain or incomplete passages are outside the clips below.

### 12:41:56 UTC, 7116 kHz

Two senders near 743 Hz have particularly regular timing, approximately 60 and 58 ms per dit. The first signs N8CT DE WA3UCR K. The conversation covers a shower, going shopping, and a video of a rotor tracking an aircraft at 41K altitude.

At 19.75–20.28 s the groups are E followed by S. A narrow measurement centered on the middle dit of S shows about one third the amplitude of the adjacent dits, still well above the nearby noise. Omitting it turns ES into EEE. The same timing check helps resolve OUT, FER, TOMORROW and PLANES. PRECISSION has a second S in the transmitted marks. After it I hear what is probably DEVICE, BUT IT WAS COOL. Several decisive cells approach the noise floor, so scoring resumes at TO WATCH. I also excluded an uncertain name after OK, a faded group before TINKERING, and the clipped ending.

### 12:46:25 UTC, 7031 kHz

The carrier is near 740 Hz. Dits last about 60–70 ms, with element gaps close to 40 ms. The operator gives the temperature twice as 32F, describes a Heathkit DX60 and VFO, and mentions making 20-meter DX contacts.

At 93.91, 94.18, 94.42 and 94.67 s there are four complete dits. Their intervening gaps measure about 197, 178 and 183 ms. Immediately afterward, the H of HEATHKIT has four much more closely spaced dits. I transcribed EEEE and the restarted word. The repeated HEE elsewhere has the same distinct letter spacing. The gaps between ordinary words are much less consistent.

Some other passages sound like OPERATING, THIS RIG and DRY JOINT IN THE VFO. I left ambiguously keyed parts of those phrases out of the reference, along with a false start on the radio model, a passage before the repeated 20M, and the final clipped mark.

## Decoder comparison

Every clip starts with a fresh decoder. Both versions receive the same audio in 20 ms blocks with automatic tuning between 400 and 1000 Hz. The earlier version is Git revision `3463ef4`. These recordings were used during development.

The updated decoder recovers a weak mark when its timing fits the sender and a narrower filter confirms energy at the carrier. It also uses rising edges to resolve a startup speed ambiguity and learns unusually wide word spacing. Across the morning clips, 21 of the remaining 23 edits are spaces. The two keyed symbol substitutions occur in DID (copied as N ID) and SHOPPING (copied as SHQPPING). The original audio SNR is unknown.

The complete database covers 759.8 seconds, including 531.2 seconds of the three morning recordings. With an additional receiver-output gain variation, the full-corpus score improved from 79 to 52 character edits. On an independently generated synthetic case with short deep fades before receiver AGC, development errors fell from 71 to 32 per 144 characters and validation errors from 102 to 42 per 145. The earlier synthetic validation aggregate stayed at 466 edits per 2,710 characters. W1AW scored 0/315 on original practice audio and 16/315 with added fade and noise in both versions.

Desktop Chrome processed each complete morning recording in its bundled worker at about 0.9% of playback time. With a 6× DevTools slowdown on the page thread, processing took 7.4–7.6% of real time and the 99th percentile was at most 9.4 ms per 20 ms audio block. A physical phone was not measured.
